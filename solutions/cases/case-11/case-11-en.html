Real-world case #11 · CLOSED-LOOP AUTOMATION

Accounts receivable that drives itself to resolution

The system checks payment status in SAP or 1C, identifies past-due invoices, determines the next action, prepares the right customer communication, asks the account manager for approval when needed, remembers deferred decisions, tracks promises to pay, reacts to partial payments, and closes the case automatically once the balance is fully settled.

01 Detect Automatically identifies a past-due invoice.
02 Prepare Selects the right scenario and prepares the message.
03 Approve Critical decisions remain with a person.
04 Monitor Tracks payments, deadlines, and customer responses.
05 Escalate Moves to the next level based on defined rules.
06 Close Automatically completes the cycle once payment is confirmed.
BEFORE Manual AR follow-up

The account manager opens the accounting system, searches for unpaid invoices, checks email, tries to remember previous conversations, writes another follow-up, sets a personal reminder, and repeats the same process a few days later.

  • some overdue invoices are noticed too late;
  • customer payment promises get buried in email threads;
  • the manager has to remember every follow-up;
  • each employee communicates with customers differently;
  • a postponed decision can easily be forgotten;
  • partial payments require manual recalculation;
  • there is no single history of decisions and actions;
  • escalation depends on one person's memory;
  • even after payment, the case may still need to be closed manually.
AFTER A controlled closed-loop workflow

The system understands what happened, prepares the next appropriate action, and brings in the account manager only when a human decision is actually required.

  • checks invoice and payment status in SAP or 1C;
  • calculates days past due;
  • selects the approved communication template;
  • inserts current invoice details and the outstanding balance;
  • requests manager approval where required;
  • records the manager's decision in the history;
  • automatically returns to deferred decisions on the right date;
  • tracks the customer's promise-to-pay date;
  • recalculates the balance after a partial payment;
  • stops automation when a dispute requires human judgment;
  • automatically closes the case once payment is received in full.
